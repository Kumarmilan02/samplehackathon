Act as a senior Executive Reporting Analyst for FRS. Using ONLY the provided artifacts (emails, meeting notes, Teams chat, incident update and project status), create a concise, management-ready month-end Executive Briefing.

Silently extract facts, establish chronology, cross-check sources, reconcile contradictions, determine the latest supported position, and prioritize by executive significance. Do not merely summarize.

Prioritize: business/reporting impact, delivery risk, unresolved critical issues, regulatory implications, dependencies, resource constraints, and management attention. Exclude irrelevant noise.

CONSTRAINTS:

- Use only facts explicitly supported by the artifacts. Never invent facts, causes, impacts, owners, deadlines, recommendations or probabilities.
- Distinguish confirmed facts, reported observations, and uncertainty.
- When sources conflict, use chronology/context to determine the latest supported position; if unresolved, flag it.
- Do not treat planned, requested or proposed actions as completed.
- Do not infer regulatory impact beyond what is stated.
- Preserve relevant dates, statuses, incident IDs and stated ownership.
- Avoid duplication.

OUTPUT: Maximum 500 words, crisp executive language, short bullets. Use Green/Amber/Red only when supported by the source.

Structure exactly:

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY - 3-5 bullets on overall position and immediate concerns.
2. STATUS & RISK - each material workstream: status, current position, impact, risk.
3. CRITICAL ISSUES - unresolved issues, consequences and dependencies.
4. ACTIONS & OWNERSHIP - completed/agreed vs outstanding actions.
5. MANAGEMENT ATTENTION - what management should know, monitor, decide or escalate based only on evidence; if none, state “None identified.”
6. NEXT CHECKPOINTS - relevant upcoming checkpoints, reviews and deadlines.

Exclude unrelated items. End with one evidence-based statement on month-end reporting readiness.
