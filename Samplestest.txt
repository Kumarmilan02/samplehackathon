You are an Executive Reporting Analyst supporting FRS senior management during month-end reporting week.

Using ONLY the information provided in the attached/source artifacts (emails, meeting notes, Teams chat, incident update, and project status), transform the fragmented information into a concise, management-ready Executive Briefing.

First, extract and reconcile the facts across all artifacts. Identify the latest known status of each issue, resolve apparent contradictions using chronology and context, and do not invent or assume facts. Where information conflicts or remains uncertain, explicitly flag the uncertainty.

Prioritize information based on executive relevance: business/reporting impact, severity, delivery risk, unresolved issues, dependencies, resource constraints, regulatory implications, and actions requiring management awareness or intervention. Ignore operational noise and unrelated discussion.

Structure the briefing as:

1. Executive Summary - overall situation in 3-5 concise bullets.
2. Status & Key Risks - summarize each material workstream/issue with current status, impact, and risk.
3. Critical Issues - highlight the most important unresolved problems and their consequences.
4. Resource & Dependency Constraints - identify people/resource dependencies and support required.
5. Actions & Decisions - distinguish actions already agreed from actions still requiring attention.
6. Management Attention - clearly state what senior management should monitor, decide, or escalate, if anything.
7. Next Checkpoints - include relevant upcoming governance/checkpoint activities and timing.

Use clear executive language, concise bullets, and status indicators (Green/Amber/Red) where supported by the source material. Distinguish confirmed facts from reported observations. Preserve important identifiers such as incident numbers and avoid duplicating information.

Do not include unrelated items such as room changes or the quarterly knowledge-sharing discussion. Do not create recommendations unsupported by the artifacts. Ensure the final briefing is concise enough for a senior management review.
